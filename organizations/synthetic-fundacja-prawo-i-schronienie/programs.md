- **Temporary protection advice and status renewal** — the highest-volume work. Renewals, documentation, PESEL registration questions, access to healthcare and schooling, and employment. Administrative rather than adversarial, and enormous in aggregate.

- **Migrant employment rights** — unpaid wages, unlawful conditions, and cases where enforcing a right risks the permit that depends on the job.

## The 2022 discontinuity, and why it matters for a data corpus

Before 2022 this organization was small and asylum-focused, handling perhaps 300 matters a year. From 2022 its volume increased roughly eightfold, its dominant client group changed, and its work shifted from adversarial procedure toward administrative advice at scale.

**It is functionally a different organization operating under the same registration.**

Worth flagging because a corpus that stores an organization's programmes as a current-state list **cannot represent this**. Anything comparing this organization to its own history, or reading a trend across its reported figures, will produce nonsense across the 2022 boundary. There is no field in the current bundle structure for "the shape of this organization changed on a date," and this bundle is the clearest case in the collection for wanting one.

The same issue exists in smaller form elsewhere — [Sierra Foothills](../synthetic-sierra-foothills-community-health/programs.md) absorbed a clinic in 2023 — but here it is the central fact.

## Which of these should become its own file first

**Temporary protection advice.** It is the majority of the organization's volume, it has its own funding, its own procedures, and its own approaching cliff — a temporary framework in its fourth year, whose eventual end will require the organization to do something it has never done at this scale. It is one bullet in a list of seven.

**Border cases** is the second candidate, for the opposite reason: very few matters, disproportionate legal and reputational weight, distinct risk to staff, and its own confidentiality considerations.

## A note on funding that has no US analogue

Part of this organization's individual support arrives through the **1% mechanism** — Polish taxpayers may designate 1% of their income tax to a specified OPP. It behaves unlike either American individual giving or government funding: it is annual, seasonal, driven by a designation campaign, and the organization does not know who its donors are.

That last part matters for anyone modelling nonprofit finance across jurisdictions. **A funding stream can be substantial and completely non-cultivatable** — there is no donor record, no stewardship, no renewal relationship. It also means this organization's "individual giving" figure cannot be compared with a US organization's without knowing that.
