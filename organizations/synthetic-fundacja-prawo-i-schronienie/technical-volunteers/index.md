# Technical volunteers

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data.**

A bundle with two kinds of thing:

- [constraints](constraints.md) — **the org's own** rules for technology volunteers. Here they are substantially **legal obligations under GDPR**, which means a volunteer is likely a *processor* or a person acting under the organization's authority, and that has contractual consequences.
- **Requests** — one file per project, each a draft the org owns and can post:
  - [website-gdpr-remediation](website-gdpr-remediation.md) — stop an asylum applicant's account of persecution being transferred to a US processor every time someone fills in the contact form, then document the processing that should have been documented years ago.

**Method:** to turn this bundle into a request, an agent reads the org's [README](../../../README.md), [technology](../technology/index.md) (inventory + capability), and [constraints](constraints.md), then frames a project grounded in the org's actual gaps — and marks it a draft the org edits and blesses before anything goes out.

## What a US-trained scoping agent will get wrong here

Everything about this organization's technology reads as competent, because it is. Managed identity, EU data residency, endpoint encryption, an EU-hosted case management platform with a DPA, a designated DPO, a well-maintained multilingual website. An agent looking for weakness will struggle to find any.

**The organization is nonetheless in ongoing breach of data protection law**, and the mechanism is a website contact form posting to a US processor — installed in 2021 by a contractor using the ordinary defaults of the web industry, which are American.

A scoping agent that reads this bundle and recommends a CRM, better analytics, or a maturity improvement plan has missed a live legal exposure affecting people in the asylum procedure. **The finding requires knowing that GDPR exists and that the rubric does not ask about it.** That is the test this bundle sets.
