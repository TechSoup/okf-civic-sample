**Overall: low-to-moderate maturity.** Generated recommendations would include: consolidate field data into a central database, publish a public monitoring map as peer organizations do, add analytics to understand your audience, and reduce reliance on paper.

## Every one of those recommendations is dangerous

Not suboptimal. **Dangerous**, in the sense that following them could contribute to someone being killed.

- **"Consolidate field data into a central database."** The consolidated artefact — monitor names, coordinates, schedules, in one queryable place — is the single most dangerous document this organization could create. Its current fragmentation is a protective measure. See [inventory](inventory.md).
- **"Publish a public monitoring map."** A map of monitoring points in contested rural territory in Colombia is a map of where defenders work. Both coalition partners publish one, correctly, in their own contexts. Here it must not exist.
- **"Add analytics."** A log of who reads a defenders' organization's findings, held by a third party, in a country where those defenders are surveilled.
- **"Reduce reliance on paper."** Paper does not synchronize, does not timestamp, and does not create a location record in a cloud system. It is doing protective work.

**The rubric is not broken. It is being used outside the conditions it assumes.** It was built for organizations whose worst data outcome is an inaccurate report, and it produces confident, specific, standard advice regardless of whether those conditions hold. It has no way to ask "would consolidating this data endanger anyone," so it does not ask, and it recommends consolidation.

## Why this is the collection's limit case

Several bundles here show a rubric mis-scoring an organization. [The Law Center](../../synthetic-central-valley-farmworker-law-center/technology/capability.md) loses points for deliberate privacy choices. [Gulf Corridor's](../../synthetic-gulf-corridor-justice-project/technology/capability.md) integrity problem has no domain. [North Star's](../../synthetic-north-star-immigrant-defense/technology/capability.md) good practice reads as deficiency. [The Polish bundle](../../synthetic-fundacja-prawo-i-schronienie/technology/capability.md) gets a reassuring score while in legal breach.

**This one is the worst case, and it establishes the shape of the whole problem.** The instrument does not merely mis-measure — it **generates actionable advice that would cause harm**, with the same confidence it applies everywhere, and nothing in its output signals that it is operating outside its assumptions.

Three things follow that generalize past this bundle:

1. **An assessment instrument needs to know when to refuse.** Not a lower score, not a caveat — a refusal to score domains where it lacks the context to interpret an absence. "We cannot assess this without knowing your threat environment" is a valid and honest output.
2. **Absence is not a measurable quantity.** A missing map, a missing database, a missing analytics platform, a missing privacy policy: each can be a gap, a deliberate protection, a legal requirement, or a program decision. **The information distinguishing them is never in the signal.** Only asking recovers it, and this collection contains eight or nine cases proving it.
3. **Benchmarking within a network can amplify the error.** This organization sits in a coalition with two peers who both publish maps. A comparison across the three would flag it as lagging. It is the one that is right.
