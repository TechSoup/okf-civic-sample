## The rubric has no domain for the thing that could sink this organization

**There is no place on this table to record that 14,000 documents cited in legal proceedings have no integrity mechanism.**

It appears, faintly, as "Cybersecurity & privacy: Developing" — alongside considerations like whether staff have MFA. The two are not comparable. If an opposing expert successfully raises doubt about whether a document in this archive is authentic, the organization loses a proceeding and, more durably, loses the credibility of its whole evidence base. No amount of endpoint protection touches that.

The gap is structural rather than an oversight in the rubric. **Security rubrics are built around confidentiality**, because for most organizations the worst outcome is a leak. This organization's worst outcome is **an unprovable archive**, and the vocabulary for that — integrity, provenance, chain of custody, non-repudiation — is not in the instrument.

Three consequences:

1. **This organization would score middling and be one challenge away from a serious problem.** A composite score conveys none of the urgency.
2. **The recommendations the rubric generates would be reasonable and beside the point.** MFA, backup verification, a privacy policy — all worth doing, none of them the issue.
3. **The organization's genuine strengths are also invisible.** Its geospatial practice is excellent and its scientific staff are the best-trained in this collection. "Staff digital skills: Established" does not convey that two people here can build a defensible dispersion analysis.
