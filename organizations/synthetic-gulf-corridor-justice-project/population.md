# Who Gulf Corridor serves

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data.**

Gulf Corridor works with residents of a **stretch of the Louisiana river corridor** — several communities across New Orleans' eastern edge and the adjacent river parishes, in an area where housing, rail, and permitted industrial facilities occupy the same ground. About **31,000 people** live inside the area the organization monitors. Many of the communities are majority Black and long-established, in some cases predating the facilities beside them by generations. *(**derived**, simulated.)*

## The population is defined by exposure, and the organization does not enroll anyone

Like [its coalition partner in Detroit](../synthetic-riverbend-air-alliance/population.md), this organization has no intake, no eligibility, and no participant list. If you live in the corridor, the work is for you.

The consequences are the same and worth restating because they recur: **participant-style metrics do not exist and cannot be derived.** What exists is geographic and activity-based — households surveyed, samples collected, comments filed, residents who testified. A funder requiring an unduplicated-participant count is asking for a number that does not describe this organization's work.

## One sub-population is recorded in detail, and it is the sensitive one

**Health survey respondents.** Several thousand households have answered a door-to-door survey about respiratory symptoms, cancer history in the household, pregnancy outcomes, and time lived at the address.

That is **health information about identifiable people, at known addresses, collected by community members rather than clinicians, held by an advocacy organization.** It is the most sensitive data in this bundle by a wide margin, and its handling requirements are unusual in three ways:

**It is not covered by HIPAA.** The organization is not a covered entity, so the framework that would govern this data at [a clinic](../synthetic-sierra-foothills-community-health/population.md) does not apply. The obligation is ethical rather than statutory, which means there is no external structure enforcing it and the organization has to construct its own.

**It is discoverable.** Unlike a clinic's records or a law office's files, this data has no privilege attached. In litigation, opposing counsel can seek it, and the organization has to assume it may one day have to produce respondent-level information about people who answered a survey at their front door. Whether respondents understood that when they consented is a live question the organization takes seriously.

**Aggregate publication can still identify people.** In a small community, "three households on this block reported a childhood cancer" is not anonymous. The organization suppresses small cells and has occasionally declined to publish a finding because it could not be reported safely.

## What the organization tells respondents

Consent is obtained in writing and the organization revised its consent language in 2023 to say plainly that survey responses **may be sought in litigation and might not be able to be kept confidential**. Response rates fell after the change. The organization kept it.

Worth recording because it is a real trade an advocacy organization faces and rarely documents: **honest consent costs data.** A bundle that showed only the response rate would show a programme getting worse.
