# Volunteer constraints & preferences

## On background checks — read this before scoping anything

A third of our participants are returning from incarceration. We spend a considerable part of our week persuading employers that a conviction record is not a character assessment and that a person who has done time can be trusted with tools, a van, and a customer's home.

**We are not going to turn around and apply a blanket background-check screen to our volunteers.** It would be incoherent, our participants would notice, and they would be right to.

What we do instead:

- **Access is scoped to the work, for everyone.** Nobody gets access to participant records because of who they are; they get it because a specific task requires it, at the minimum level that task needs, for as long as it needs. That rule applies identically to a volunteer, a new staff member, and a board member.
- **A signed confidentiality agreement is required** from anyone who will be near participant data. This is firm, and it is the control we actually rely on.
- **Conviction-history fields are the tightest tier we have.** For any volunteer, the default is **no access at all** — work should be scoped so those fields are never in view. Where a data-migration task genuinely cannot avoid them, we want them **pseudonymized or masked in the working environment**, and we want the reason written down.
- **We do ask about relevant history, and we ask it narrowly.** For a volunteer whose work would put them in unsupervised access to financial systems, we ask about financial-crime history — because it is relevant to that specific work. We do not ask people about their entire past because they offered to help with a database.
- **We will say no to a volunteer whose framing is wrong.** Someone who describes our participants as risks, or who wants to build "flags" into a system, is not a fit here regardless of their technical skill. This has happened.

If a placement service or a volunteer's employer requires a check we don't, that is between them and their policy — we will not pretend it is ours.

## Everything else

- **The legacy database is fragile and holds the sensitive material.** Assume it is not backed up until someone proves otherwise. Nothing destructive, nothing in place, no "quick cleanup." Work from a copy, always.
- **Do not build participant-facing risk scores, flags, or predictive anything.** Not "likelihood to complete," not "employability scoring," not a model trained on who got placed. We have seen what those do to who gets offered which track, and we will not have one. This is a hard no and it applies to any use of AI on participant data.
- **Government reporting must not break.** Three funders and a state portal depend on data this project touches. A reporting cycle missed because of a migration is a real financial consequence. Cut over between reporting periods or not at all.
- **Wage, benefits, and drug-screen data are in scope for protection, not for tidying.** They exist because employers require them. Volunteers should treat them as radioactive and leave them where they are unless the project specifically requires otherwise.
- **Remote is fine for most work.** The legacy machine is on-site and cannot leave, so anything touching it directly requires being here, escorted, during business hours.
- **Availability:** program staff are teaching. Realistic windows are early mornings, Fridays, and the two-week gaps between cohorts. A volunteer who needs a program manager's sustained attention mid-cohort will not get it.
- **Handover:** we have 34 staff and no IT department. The one person who understands the legacy database is the reason it still runs, and we are not interested in acquiring a second system like that. Documentation a competent non-specialist can follow is a deliverable, not a nicety.
- **Eligibility limits:** as above — scoped access and a confidentiality agreement, not a categorical screen. If that rules out a volunteer whose own policy requires otherwise, so be it.
