# Programs & services

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data.**

*The list below is **derived** (simulated) from public materials; a real organization would confirm and replace it with its own description.*

Valle Verde's programming is nine routes and the things that hang off them.

- **Mobile pantry routes** — nine routes on a two-week cycle, reaching 17 unincorporated communities. Four vehicles, a paid driver-coordinator each, and volunteers who load at the warehouse. This is the organization; everything else attaches to it.
- **Produce recovery** — collecting from regional packing houses fruit and vegetables that fail cosmetic grading but are perfectly good food. Highly seasonal, occasionally overwhelming: a donated forty-bin lot of stone fruit is a gift and a logistics emergency at the same time.
- **Promotora program** — eleven community health and outreach workers from the communities they serve, speaking Spanish, Mixtec, or Triqui. They carry information about the pantry, and increasingly about everything else, which is both the program's strength and a load problem nobody has solved.
- **Bottled water distribution** — for communities whose domestic wells fail nitrate or arsenic standards. The organization is candid that this is a stopgap for an infrastructure failure it cannot fix and did not cause.
- **Nutrition and preparation sessions** — run by promotoras, oral and demonstration-based rather than handout-based, keyed to whatever the recovery stream produced that month.
- **Referral to legal services** — an advocate from [synthetic-Central Valley Farmworker Law Center](../synthetic-central-valley-farmworker-law-center/README.md) rides two routes a month. Wage theft and housing conditions surface in the pantry line before they surface anywhere else.

*(Food distribution is the activity behind its [K30](../../_shared/ntee/K30.md) classification; the warehouse and recovery operation is why it also carries [K31](../../_shared/ntee/K31.md).)*

## Which of these should become its own file first

**Bottled water distribution.** It has nothing to do with food, it exists because of a drinking-water infrastructure failure, it has its own funder and its own end condition — the organization wants to stop doing it — and describing it as a food program obscures what it actually is. It is also the program most likely to be misclassified by anything reading this bundle, since the organization's classification codes are all about food.

The **promotora program** is the second candidate, because it has quietly become the organization's most valuable asset and its least defined one. It is currently a bullet inside a food program and it is functioning as the trusted communications infrastructure for seventeen communities.

## A note on the route cycle

Routes run on a **two-week cycle, not weekly**, and the cycle is what makes the technology problem in this bundle hard. A driver-coordinator is out of contact for most of a route day — see [inventory](technology/inventory.md) — so route data arrives in batches, on paper, up to two weeks after the fact. That lag is the reason the organization cannot see a demand spike until it has passed.
