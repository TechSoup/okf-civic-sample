## The one that matters

**Cybersecurity & privacy, At risk** — and it is worth being precise about why, because it is not carelessness.

This organization has the **same HIPAA obligations as [Sierra Foothills](../../synthetic-sierra-foothills-community-health/technology/capability.md)** — the statute does not scale with revenue. Sierra Foothills has $7.9M, contracted IT support, an EHR vendor relationship, and a compliance officer. Cumberland Gap Health has $1.1M, fourteen staff, and nobody whose job description includes the word compliance.

The obligation is identical. The capacity to meet it differs by a factor of seven. **That gap is not a finding about this organization; it is a finding about how regulatory burden lands on small rural providers**, and any assessment that scores the two side by side without saying so is producing a comparison that misleads.

## What the rubric gets right and wrong here

**Right:** the low-ish scores across the board are broadly fair. This is not a case like [the Law Center](../../synthetic-central-valley-farmworker-law-center/technology/capability.md) where deliberate choices scan as deficiencies. The organization would agree with most of this table.

**Wrong, or at least missing:** there is no domain here for *the thing that dominates the organization's technology life*, which is that most of its inbound clinical information arrives as unstructured faxes because of decisions made by other institutions. It shows up faintly under Data & CRM. It is most of a person's job, every morning, and a clinical risk when that person is on leave.

Also missing: **anything about the patients' technology**. The organization's single largest technology-shaped constraint is that 40% of its patients cannot use a video visit. That is not assessable as an organizational capability, and the rubric has no place for it, so the most important fact goes unrecorded.
