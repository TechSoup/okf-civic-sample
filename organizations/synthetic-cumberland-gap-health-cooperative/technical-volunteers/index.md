# Technical volunteers

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data.**

A bundle with two kinds of thing:

- [constraints](constraints.md) — **the org's own** rules and preferences for volunteers. As with [Sierra Foothills](../../synthetic-sierra-foothills-community-health/technical-volunteers/constraints.md), several of these are HIPAA obligations rather than preferences — but this organization has to meet them with fourteen staff and no compliance officer, which changes the practical shape of what a volunteer can do.
- **Requests** — one file per project, each a draft the org owns and can post:
  - [inbound-document-routing](inbound-document-routing.md) — stop one person spending every morning sorting faxes by hand, without letting a model decide anything clinical.

**Method:** to turn this bundle into a request, an agent reads the org's [README](../../../README.md), [technology](../technology/index.md) (inventory + capability), and [constraints](constraints.md), then frames a project grounded in the org's actual gaps — and marks it a draft the org edits and blesses before anything goes out.

## What a good scoping agent should notice about this bundle

The organization's stated pain is the fax pile, and the request below addresses it. But two other things in the bundle are arguably more urgent and neither is what the organization asked for:

**There is no current HIPAA risk assessment**, and no compliance officer to own one. That is a regulatory exposure, and the honest advice to a fourteen-person clinic may be that a consultant is the right answer rather than a volunteer.

**The fax server's backup status is unverified**, and it holds clinical documents that exist nowhere else the organization controls until someone files them. Same shape as the Phase 0 problem in [Motor City Trades'](../../synthetic-motor-city-trades-institute/technical-volunteers/participant-record-consolidation.md) bundle: a small, unglamorous, days-not-weeks fix that matters more than the project it's attached to.

A request that names these without hijacking the organization's own priorities is doing the job properly. The one below tries.
