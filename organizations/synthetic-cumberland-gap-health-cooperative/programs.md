# Programs & services

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data.**

*The list below is **derived** (simulated) from public materials; a real organization would confirm and replace it with its own description.*

Fourteen staff, one site, and a truck. The programme list is short because the organization is.

- **Primary care** — the clinic, five days a week, staffed by two nurse practitioners with physician coverage two days.
- **Home and community visits** — for the ~40% of patients who cannot travel. A nurse practitioner and the community health worker, in a truck, on a rotation. Operationally the most expensive thing the organization does and the least reimbursable.
- **Chronic disease management** — diabetes and hypertension, the bulk of the caseload. Includes the unglamorous work of helping patients afford medication they have been prescribed.
- **Respiratory and occupational lung disease** — pulmonary care for former miners, paired with **black lung benefits documentation and advocacy**. A clinical service and a benefits service at once.
- **Substance-use recovery support** — because nobody else in the county provides it. Small, sensitive, and the program whose confidentiality requirements shape the organization's whole approach to patient communication.
- **Medication assistance** — navigating patient-assistance programmes and discount pathways. Almost entirely paperwork, and it is a real determinant of whether a treatment plan happens.
- **Clinical placements** — hosting trainees from [Black Mountain Workforce Partnership](../synthetic-black-mountain-workforce-partnership/README.md)'s health-careers programme, and hiring some of them. This is workforce development that happens to be reciprocal: the organization's staffing pipeline and its partner's placement pipeline are the same thing.
- **Telehealth** — *listed because it exists on paper.* See below.

## Telehealth is on the list and it barely runs

The organization stood up telehealth in 2020 with grant funding, as nearly every clinic did. It kept it, and it uses it for a small number of patients where it works.

For most of its patients **it does not work, and the reason is not the organization's to fix**: the connection where they live will not carry a video call. See [population](population.md) and [inventory](technology/inventory.md).

It stays on the programme list because it is real for the patients who can use it, and because removing it would misrepresent what the organization offers. But **anything reading this bundle should not treat "has telehealth" as meaning what it usually means.** A capability that 40% of the patient population cannot access is a different kind of capability, and the flag on its own is misleading.

## Which of these should become its own file first

**Black lung benefits documentation and advocacy.** It is currently folded into a clinical service line and it is substantially a legal and administrative practice with its own timelines, its own adversary, and its own record-keeping obligations — medical records that function as evidence in a federal benefits claim have to be kept differently from ordinary clinical records. It is also the organization's most distinctive work and the hardest to see from outside.

**Home and community visits** is the second candidate, for a different reason: it is where nearly half the organization's patient contact happens, it costs the most, and it is described in one line.
