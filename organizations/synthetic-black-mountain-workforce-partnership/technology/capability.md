## The odd shape: strong infrastructure, absent data layer

This organization scores **Established on infrastructure** — better than several larger organizations in this collection — and **At risk on data**. That combination is unusual and it has a specific cause.

The infrastructure is good because **it had to be**: the organization needed a connection that could carry graduates' remote jobs, so it bought one, and it needed laptops that worked in participants' homes, so it acquired and secured them. Infrastructure was a *program requirement*, so it got program attention and program money.

The data layer had no such forcing function. Nobody's job depends on it, no participant notices it, and the reporting it feeds is produced by one person working around its absence. So it never got built.

**The lesson generalizes across this collection:** organizations build the technology their program forces them to build. [Riverbend Air](../../synthetic-riverbend-air-alliance/technology/capability.md) has excellent field instrumentation and no data stewardship. [Motor City Trades](../../synthetic-motor-city-trades-institute/technology/capability.md) has solid finance systems, because auditors ask, and terrible participant data, because nobody does. Here, infrastructure is a program input and data is not.

If you want to predict where an organization's technology will be weak, ask **which parts of it someone outside would notice were broken.** That predicts better than budget does.

## What the rubric misses here

**The workstations and the lending library are not organizational technology.** They are a small piece of public digital infrastructure that a workforce nonprofit is providing because nothing else does. The rubric counts them as assets supporting the organization's operations, which is not what they are — and it has no way to record that this organization is, incidentally, a public computing resource for part of a county.

**Concentration risk isn't a domain.** The single largest technology-adjacent risk in this bundle is that one person can produce the federal report. That is a staffing and documentation fact, it would take four days a quarter of someone's knowledge with her if she left, and there is nowhere on this rubric to say so.
