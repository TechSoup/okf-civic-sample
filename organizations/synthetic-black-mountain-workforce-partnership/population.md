# Who Black Mountain serves

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data.**

Black Mountain serves **working-age adults in Letcher County** — about **190 enrollments a year**, ages 19 to 61 with a median around 38. Most have a high school credential and no post-secondary training. A substantial share have **previous mining or mining-adjacent employment**, either directly or through a household member, and arrive with wage expectations set by a job that paid well and no longer exists. *(**corroborated**, simulated — enrollment is reported into the state workforce system and independently held.)*

## What makes this population distinct in the collection

**They have been retrained before.** A meaningful number of participants have completed at least one previous training program, sometimes two, funded by one of the transition initiatives that have passed through the region since the 2010s. Some of those programs led nowhere, through no fault of the participant.

The consequence is a population with **earned skepticism**, and it shapes the organization's whole approach: short cycles, employer commitments arranged before a cohort starts, and a refusal to run a track without a demonstrable local job at the end. The organization's own framing — see [README](README.md) — is that nobody here needs to be told to learn to code again.

Anything reading this bundle to design a program or an intervention should register that **"low engagement" in this population is not a motivation problem.** It's a track record.

## Sub-populations

**Former mining households** — the core. Often the whole household's income history is in one industry, which means a career change is a family financial event rather than an individual one.

**Adults with a conviction record** — a smaller share than at [Motor City Trades](../synthetic-motor-city-trades-institute/population.md) but present, and with a specific local problem: the healthcare tracks that offer the best local employment have licensing screens that a conviction can close. The organization is candid that it sometimes has to tell someone the track they want is not available to them, which it describes as the worst conversation in the job.

**Participants in recovery** — overlapping with the clinic's recovery caseload, and the reason the two organizations coordinate carefully. Employment is a stabilizing factor and also a stressor, and a training schedule that ignores treatment appointments will lose people.

**Participants who cannot reliably work from home** — see [README](README.md). Not a demographic so much as an infrastructure fact that determines which tracks are viable for whom, and the reason the organization built workstations in its own building.

## What is and isn't tracked

**Tracked, and audited:** enrollment, attendance, completion, credential attainment, and **placement at completion**. These go into the state workforce system, which is the system of record and is not the organization's own.

**Not tracked, and the organization says so:** whether a graduate is still in the job at six or twelve months. It does not have the staff to follow up systematically and it declines to publish a figure it cannot support. See [eligibility](verification.md) for why that honesty is costly.
