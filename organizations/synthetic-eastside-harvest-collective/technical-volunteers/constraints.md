# Volunteer constraints & preferences

> **⚠ Synthetic.** In a real bundle **this file is the organization's to edit**, and it starts from sensible defaults and diverges as the org fills it in. An agent scoping a project must treat these as non-negotiable.

*Stub — pre-seeded with defaults appropriate to an organization that employs minors and serves an open-door food program.*

- **Youth employment safeguarding (highest priority):** the farm crew includes workers as young as 16. Any volunteer whose work touches crew records — hours, wages, contact information, school status — requires a **background check**, and crew data must never leave systems the organization controls. Prefer scoping technical work so it never touches minor-employee records at all.
- **No participant identification, ever.** Distribution is open-door by design: no documentation, no names, no proof of residency. That is a **program commitment, not a data gap**. A volunteer must not build anything that would start identifying the people in the food line, however useful the resulting data would be. Aggregate counts only.
- **Anything that touches immigration status is out of scope.** The organization does not ask and will not store it. This constraint stands even if a funder requests the data.
- **Seasonal availability:** March through November the farm staff are outdoors and effectively unreachable during daylight. Project work needs winter, or evenings, and a volunteer who plans mid-July working sessions with farm managers will not get them.
- **Remote vs. on-site:** remote is fine for most work, but anyone designing data capture for the sites should visit **at least one distribution day** first. The paper notebooks make sense once you've seen the conditions they're filled out in.
- **Handover:** every project must leave documentation a non-technical staff member can follow. The organization runs lean and has no IT staff; a solution only one person understands is worse than the notebook.
