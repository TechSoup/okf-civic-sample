The Law Center's work divides into representation, outreach, and the intake that connects them.

- **Wage and hour claims** — unpaid wages, piece-rate violations, unlawful deductions, missed breaks. The largest share of the caseload and the most winnable.
- **Housing conditions** — habitability cases, often against a landlord who is also the employer, which changes the strategy considerably and raises the retaliation risk for the client.
- **Workplace safety and pesticide exposure** — smaller volume, longer timelines, and the matters most likely to require expert evidence the organization cannot easily afford.
- **Removal defense** — a small, oversubscribed practice. The organization accepts a fraction of the requests it receives.
- **Know-your-rights sessions** — held in community halls, churches, and labor camps, in Spanish with interpretation. These are outreach, not representation, and they are the organization's main source of eventual cases.
- **Tailgate intake** — advocates riding two [Valle Verde](../synthetic-valle-verde-food-network/README.md) pantry routes a month, taking intakes where people already are. The most productive intake channel the organization has.

## The structural problem inside the caseload

**The legal timeline outlasts the client's presence.** A wage claim can run eighteen months. A seasonal or H-2A worker may be in the county for five. The organization loses contact with clients mid-matter routinely, not through anyone's fault, and a matter with no reachable client is a matter that dies.

This is the operational fact that shapes the most about how the organization works, and it is worth noting that **no technology fixes it**. Better contact records help at the margin. The underlying problem is that the justice system's clock and the agricultural labor calendar are not compatible, and a case-management improvement should be honest about operating at that margin rather than claiming to solve it.

## Which of these should become its own file first

**Removal defense.** It has a different funding basis, different professional-liability exposure, different urgency, a different client relationship, and — importantly — a **capacity-rationing decision** at its center that none of the other programs have. Describing it as one bullet among six understates what it is and hides the hardest thing the organization does.

**Know-your-rights** is the second candidate, because it is currently classified as outreach and functions as the organization's intake pipeline, and those are different things with different measures of success.
