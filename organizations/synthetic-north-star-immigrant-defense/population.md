# Who North Star serves

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data.**

North Star serves **people facing removal from the United States** and their families — roughly **1,300 active matters**, drawn from across Minnesota and predominantly from the Twin Cities. Clients speak Spanish, Somali, [[Oromo]], Hmong, [[Karen]], Amharic, Arabic, and a long tail of others. Many have been in the country for years; some have been here their entire remembered lives. *(**derived**, simulated — from grant reporting, not case files, which were not examined.)*

## The population's defining feature is that it can be looked for

Most population files in this collection describe who an organization helps. This one has to start somewhere else: **the fact of being this organization's client is itself dangerous information.**

A list of North Star's clients is a list of people in removal proceedings, with addresses, in one place. There is an obvious party who would find that list useful and a range of others who would find it interesting. Nothing else in this collection has that property — [the Law Center's](../synthetic-central-valley-farmworker-law-center/population.md) clients face employer retaliation, which is serious and different; [Frogtown Table's](../synthetic-frogtown-community-table/population.md) shoppers are protected by not being recorded at all.

Consequences that run through the whole bundle:

- **The organization stores less than it could**, on purpose. See [constraints](technical-volunteers/constraints.md).
- **It publishes almost nothing** about individual clients, including success stories, unless a client specifically wants that and understands the risk.
- **It assumes any system it uses may be compelled or breached**, and asks what would be exposed if it were.

## Sub-populations

**Detained clients** — the most urgent and the hardest to serve. A detained person may be transferred out of state with little notice, cannot easily be contacted, and is on a court timeline that does not wait. Representation for detained people is partly funded by a county contract.

**Unaccompanied minors** — children in proceedings without a parent or guardian. Their cases involve additional protections, additional confidentiality, and a longer arc.

**Families with mixed status** — the most common shape. A matter about one person is a matter affecting a household where different members have different status and different exposure. Anything the organization sends to a home address is read by people whose situations differ.

**Rapid-response callers** — people or their relatives calling in the hours after a detention. The organization's front line, and the population it most often cannot help. See below.

**Pro bono clients** — matters carried by one of the ~90 private-practice attorneys in the organization's network, under supervision. From the client's point of view they are North Star's client; in the data they are somewhere less clear, which is the [volunteer project](technical-volunteers/index.md).

## The number the organization won't publish

North Star tracks how many rapid-response calls it cannot take. It does not report the figure.

The reasoning: a published number showing that most callers get no lawyer would **discourage people from calling**, and a call that doesn't happen is a person who definitely gets no representation. The organization would rather absorb the reputational cost of appearing not to measure its capacity than deter the calls.

**So the most meaningful measure of unmet need in this population is deliberately absent from every public source.** For anyone building need models, coverage maps, or capacity analyses from published nonprofit data: the gap here is not a data-quality problem to be worked around. It is a judgement made by the people closest to the harm, and it is probably right.
