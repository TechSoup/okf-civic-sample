## The pro bono network is the organization's capacity, and its blind spot

Ninety outside attorneys carrying North Star cases under supervision. This is genuinely the difference between the organization serving 1,300 matters and serving perhaps 400.

It is also **tracked separately from the cases themselves**. The case management system holds matters; a set of spreadsheets holds the attorney network — who has been trained, who is available, who has capacity, who has taken what. Connecting the two is done by people who remember.

The consequences are ordinary and cumulative: a supervising attorney cannot quickly see everything one pro bono attorney is carrying; nobody can reliably tell which network members are active this quarter; a pro bono attorney's client sometimes calls North Star and reaches someone who cannot find their matter. See [inventory](technology/inventory.md) — this is the [volunteer project](technical-volunteers/index.md).

## The rapid-response line is a single point of failure

24-hour coverage, staffed on rotation by people who have day jobs at the same organization. It runs on a forwarding arrangement and a shared log. When it works, it works because a specific person picked up at 2 a.m.

The organization knows this is fragile. It also knows the alternative — an answering service, a triage script, a callback queue — puts a layer between a frightened caller and a lawyer at the moment that layer costs the most. It has chosen fragility with a human on the end, deliberately, and would defend the choice.

## Which of these should become its own file first

**The pro bono network.** It is currently a bullet and it is a program with its own recruitment, training curriculum, supervision structure, malpractice considerations, and roughly two-thirds of the organization's service capacity attached to it. Describing it as one line understates it more than anything else in this bundle.

**The rapid-response line** is the second candidate, for the opposite reason: it is tiny, it is fragile, and it is where the organization's hardest capacity decisions get made at three in the morning. It deserves a file that says so.
