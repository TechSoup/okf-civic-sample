# Who Career Lab serves

**⚠ Synthetic — fabricated data. And note that this bundle's [verification determination has expired](verification.md).**

Career Lab serves **working adults in New Orleans changing careers** — about **260 enrollments a year**, ages 21 to 58 with a median around 33. The defining characteristic is that **most are employed while they train**, typically in hospitality, food service, retail, or gig work, and typically on schedules that shift week to week. *(**corroborated**, simulated — enrollment is reported into the state workforce system.)*

## Everything about this population follows from one fact

**They cannot quit their jobs to train.**

That is not a preference or a motivation issue. A household with no savings and rent due cannot take twelve unpaid weeks, and a hospitality schedule is not something a worker controls — a shift can move with two days' notice. So:

- **All instruction is evenings and weekends**, and even then attendance is interrupted by shift changes nobody chose.
- **Attrition is concentrated in weeks four to seven**, when the accumulated cost of two jobs becomes unsustainable. The organization knows this precisely and can do relatively little about it.
- **"Completion" measures endurance as much as learning.** The organization is fairly candid that some of the people who dropped out had learned the material and ran out of capacity to keep showing up.
- **Storm season disrupts everything.** Late summer cohorts lose weeks. The organization builds slack into the calendar and it is not always enough.

Anything reading this bundle to interpret completion rates should hold that. A completion figure here is not comparable to one from a program whose participants train full-time, and comparing them without adjustment will make this organization look worse than a daytime program with a more secure population.

## Sub-populations

**Hospitality and service workers** — the core, and the source of the schedule problem. Many have substantial workplace skill that no credential records.

**Parents on a hard clock** — evening classes and childcare are in direct competition. The organization provides some childcare support and describes it as the intervention with the clearest effect on completion, and the hardest to fund because it looks like overhead.

**Participants in the green-infrastructure track** — a newer group, coming partly through [Gulf Corridor](../synthetic-gulf-corridor-justice-project/README.md)'s corridor communities, where the stated principle is that people living with the pollution should get the remediation jobs.

**Participants displaced by a storm** — recurring rather than exceptional. Some arrive because a storm ended a job; some disappear mid-cohort because a storm ended a home.

## What is tracked

Enrollment, attendance, completion, credential attainment, and **placement at completion** — reported into the state workforce system, which is the system of record.

**Wage-gain data is collected and is patchy.** The organization tries to record starting wage before and after, because a placement into a job paying the same as the one someone left is not a success. It gets the figure for maybe half of placements. It reports it with that caveat, which is more careful than [one other workforce organization in this collection](../synthetic-motor-city-trades-institute/population.md) and less complete than it would like.
