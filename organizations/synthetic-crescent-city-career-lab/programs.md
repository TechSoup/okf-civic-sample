## Which of these should become its own file first

**Wraparound supports, and specifically childcare.** The organization's own analysis says it drives completion more than any instructional change, and it is one bullet inside a list of six. Giving it a file would make it visible as the intervention it is, with its own costs, its own participant numbers, and its own funding problem — which is that the thing that works best looks least like a program.

**Storm-season contingency** is the second candidate, for a reason specific to this city: it is treated as an operational adjustment and it is closer to a standing program with a predictable annual cycle, its own re-entry pathway, and a real effect on the organization's reported completion figures.

## A note on employer partners

Thirty is the number the organization reports. It appears to include employers who hire from every cohort and employers who took one graduate in 2023, with **no definition distinguishing them.**

This is worth flagging as a general data problem rather than a criticism. "Employer partner" has no standard meaning, grant applications ask for a count, and a count is what gets produced. The consequence is that the most important question about a workforce organization — will anyone actually hire these graduates — is answered with a figure that mixes a pipeline and a one-off.

If you are comparing workforce organizations across this collection, note that [Motor City Trades](../synthetic-motor-city-trades-institute/programs.md) reports "two union halls and eleven employers" and [Black Mountain](../synthetic-black-mountain-workforce-partnership/programs.md) reports employer commitments arranged before each cohort starts. **Three organizations, three incompatible notions of an employer relationship, all reported as counts.**
